- TBD: better clothing fit. `[[clothing]]` binds each clothing vertex to the bones of the nearest body vertex in the rest pose, cuts the clothing to a height range (`keep`) and pushes points inside the body out along the skin normal (`gap`). Tried with "Female Sci-Fi Cloth" on the Cyborg girl (made for another body; `keep = [420, 1260]`, `offset = [0, -130, 0]`, `gap = 6`), not committed:
  - `hug`, pulling points farther than a distance from the skin onto it: removes floating parts, but flattens voluminous parts such as the belt and breaks straight seams, because each point moves along the normal of a different body vertex.
  - Hiding body triangles under the clothing (all three vertices within a distance of a clothing point), so skin cannot show through between sparse clothing points (e.g. at the buttocks, where the body is rounder than the clothing): by plain distance it also cut the upper back and the arms next to the top; requiring the clothing point to lie outwards along the body normal, or not on an open edge of the clothing, or near the body line, each fixed one region and broke another. An untried variant: count a clothing point only if the body vertex it follows is near the hidden one.
  - Bevy pitfalls: a skinned mesh given a new mesh asset keeps the old bounding box (use `NoFrustumCulling`); edit a body mesh asset in place instead of swapping its `Mesh3d`; nearest-point searches in a grid need a bounded radius, or they stall on points far from any clothing.
  - A rigid fix per part (move or scale one piece) needs the parts as separate meshes; here the collar, shorts and shin guards are one mesh.
  - Clothing made for the avatar's own base body (same mesh and skeleton) avoids all of this and is the better source.
- TBD: exact Unity humanoid clips without Unity. `riggy install` converts a `.unitypackage`'s muscle clips on a fixed test rig from unity-asset-reader, so a clip's trunk and head can differ by a few degrees from how it plays on the pack's own character. Exact results need the Avatar Unity computes when it imports the pack's character (`PreQ`/`PostQ` axes, limit signs, the body's rest tilt `m_RootX`); the package holds only the FBX and its `.meta` (bone mapping, limits). No open implementation of that import step was found (2026-11-15: ShaderMotion, lyuma's VRMChecker gist, gmconverter, reze-rig and AssetRipper all read a Unity-built Avatar). Reverse engineering it can be checked bone by bone: `temp/2026-11-15_basic_motions/` holds the Basic Motions demo character's Unity-built Avatar (`unity_ref/bundle/`), Unity's playback of three clips (`unity_ref/animator/`) and the comparison scripts (`cmp/`).
